The pitch in one line: M.A.C.H. is a shop-floor assistant that answers from the plant's own SOPs, diagrams and tables, cites the exact step it used, refuses or calls an expert when unsure, and never answers from a superseded revision.

Why it can win. Image-based document search and self-checking RAG are already published at ICLR and NAACL, so on their own they are table stakes. What we found no peer-reviewed work on is the trust layer for safety-critical procedures: step-level revision safety (outdated documents in general are studied, procedures are not), step-level safety-complete citations, hazard-tiered abstention, and AI drafts measured under change control. That layer is M.A.C.H.'s novelty. Every claim below is worded "we found no prior work", not "world first", because IEEE and Elsevier full texts were checked through the OpenAlex index, not read in full.

1. The problem

Operators follow SOPs and work instructions (WIs) that change through revisions, engineering change notices (ECNs) and temporary deviations. Key values (torques, temperatures, sequences) often live in tables and diagrams, not text. Keyword search misses those. Plain chatbots and plain RAG add a new risk: a fluent, confident, wrong answer.

  • Outdated documents flip answers. One stale document overturns 17–91% of otherwise-correct RAG answers arXiv 2026, not peer-reviewed. Date-based re-ranking recovers only 4.6–10 points; stating explicitly when old evidence stops applying fixes most of it. Peer-reviewed work confirms the harm HoH, ACL 2025 and shows version-unaware evaluation misreports recall by up to 25.7 points EMNLP 2026 Industry, but neither covers procedures.
  • Citations are not proof. Commercial legal RAG tools hallucinate on 17–33% of queries, including citations that don't support the claim J. Empirical Legal Studies 2025. In medicine, 50–90% of LLM answers are not fully supported by their own sources Nature Communications 2025.
  • Industry has no benchmark. The peer-reviewed manufacturing RAG papers we found evaluate retrieval only Procedia CIRP 2025. Nobody has published a hallucination audit of generative RAG on SOPs.
  • Auditors care. ISO 9001 §7.5 requires that only the current revision is available at the point of use; an obsolete WI at a station is a common nonconformity. 21 CFR 211.100(b) requires every deviation to be recorded and justified.

3. System design

M.A.C.H. has three parts. Offline ingestion turns documents into searchable, revision-tagged pieces. The online answer path runs every question through a chain of checks before anything reaches the operator. Governance handles experts, change control and the audit trail.

The checks every answer passes

  1. Context lock. Role, station, machine model, serial range and shift are attached to the question, so search can only see documents the user may read and that apply to their machine.
  2. Revision Guard. A hard filter, not a soft re-rank: only chunks whose status is effective, whose date window covers today and whose scope covers the operator's line are searchable. Deviations and ECNs are applied to the specific steps they patch.
  3. Evidence grading. A CRAG-style grader scores what was found. Ambiguous evidence triggers up to two re-searches; irrelevant evidence leads to abstention.
  4. Conflict detection. If two active sources give different values for the same parameter, M.A.C.H. shows both and escalates. A quality alert outranks the SOP until it is resolved.
  5. Number lock. Every torque, temperature, pressure or dimension in the answer is copied by code from the cited table cell, with tolerance and unit. If OCR text and the image reading disagree on a digit, the system abstains.
  6. Citation and safety verification. Each step's citation must entail the step, all citations must come from one revision set and the cited variant must match the machine, and every warning, PPE or lockout/tagout (LOTO) step in the source must appear in the answer.
  7. Hazard-tiered gate. The confidence needed to answer depends on the step's hazard tier (below). Below threshold, the system gives a partial answer or abstains, then escalates.
Hazard tierExamplesTarget max error on answered stepsBelow threshold
Tier 1 · CriticalLOTO, pressure release, lifting, torque on safety fasteners≤ 1%Abstain, "stop and hold", escalate
Tier 2 · QualityTorque specs, temperatures, inspection criteria≤ 5%Partial answer, escalate the uncertain steps
Tier 3 · GeneralTool locations, part descriptions, cosmetic steps≤ 10%Answer with a "low confidence" label

Thresholds are calibrated on a held-out set with conformal risk control (Mohri & Hashimoto, ICML 2024; C-RAG, ICML 2024), separately per tier. They are re-checked whenever an SOP family gets a new revision, because the statistical guarantee assumes new questions look like the calibration set. The error targets are our design choice, not values from a paper.

6. Evaluation plan

We compare five systems on the same frozen corpus, with the same LLM, top-k and context budget, so any gain comes from M.A.C.H.'s design.

Systems

  1. BM25 keyword search (shows top pages only)
  2. BM25 + LLM
  3. Text-only RAG (dense over OCR text, no checks)
  4. Hybrid text RAG (BM25 + dense, RRF)
  5. M.A.C.H. full, plus ablations: no page images, no Revision Guard, no verifier, no hazard gate, no router

Metrics

  • Retrieval: Recall@5, nDCG@10 (multi-gold)
  • Answers: correctness, faithfulness, citation precision/recall (ALCE-style)
  • Procedure: step-order fidelity, exact match on critical values, safety-step recall
  • Trust: revision-mixing rate, superseded-citation rate, abstention precision/recall, unsafe-answer rate, escalation rate
  • Drafting: accept/edit/reject, edit distance, planted-fault catch rate
  • Ops: p50/p95 latency, cost per query

Making it rigorous

  • Corpus: real revision chains from public FAA Airworthiness Directive supersessions, plus scripted counterfactual edits and a realistic shop-floor set (SOPs with 2–3 revisions, ECNs, deviations, quality alerts, RCAs, drawings, photos). Freeze and hash the snapshot.
  • Labels: each question has a gold answer, all valid evidence locations, expected behaviour (answer, abstain or escalate) and, for procedures, the ordered steps and critical values. Two annotators label at least 25%; report Cohen's κ.
  • Split: 30% dev for tuning prompts and thresholds, 70% test touched once.
  • Judging: an LLM judge from a different model family than the generator, run 3 times with shuffled order, validated on ≥100 human-labelled items. Critical values are checked by exact match, never by the judge.
  • Statistics: paired per-question tests (McNemar or bootstrap), Holm correction across metrics, 95% confidence intervals; ARES-style correction to turn a small human-labelled set into intervals.
  • Human study: 3–5 people answer 10 questions with keyword search vs M.A.C.H.; report minutes per correct answer.

9. Jury questions and our answers

Q1. Isn't this just RAG with extra steps?
RAG is the base. The contribution is the trust layer: Revision Guard, step-level safety-complete citations, number lock and hazard-tiered abstention. We found no peer-reviewed work on these for procedures, and we measure each with an ablation.
Q2. How do you know which revision is effective?
From document-control metadata (status, effective dates, scope, supersedes links), not file dates. A hard filter removes everything else before the LLM sees it, and the verifier rejects any answer mixing revisions.
Q3. What if two current documents disagree?
M.A.C.H. never picks silently. It shows both sources, escalates to an expert, and treats a quality alert as overriding the SOP until resolved.
Q4. How do you stop hallucinated numbers?
Number lock: critical values are copied by code from the cited table cell with their tolerance and unit. The LLM never writes them. If OCR and the image reading disagree, the system abstains.
Q5. Won't it refuse too often to be useful?
We report the risk–coverage curve and escalation rate, not just accuracy. Only tier-1 hazardous steps need very high confidence; general lookups answer with a confidence label. Partial answers keep the operator moving.
Q6. Where do confidence thresholds come from?
Conformal risk control on a held-out calibration set, separately per hazard tier (ICML 2024 methods). Thresholds are re-checked whenever an SOP family is revised.
Q7. Why not fine-tune a model on the SOPs?
Retrieval beats fine-tuning for adding new knowledge (Ovadia et al., EMNLP 2024), and SOPs change too often. Updating an index is instant and auditable; retraining is neither.
Q8. You have no real plant data. Is the test valid?
Part of it is real: revision chains come from public FAA Airworthiness Directive supersessions, and we also run eight public peer-reviewed benchmarks. The shop-floor part is synthetic but reviewed by someone with plant experience (name them). The benchmark, code and schema are designed so LTTS can drop in real documents and rerun the same evaluation.
Q9. Can a malicious document hijack it?
Instruction-like text is stripped at ingestion, retrieved text is treated as data, output is schema-enforced, and access is filtered by role before search. We include injection documents in the test set.
Q10. What if the cloud LLM is down or data can't leave the plant?
The reference configuration already runs on-prem: a 4-bit 7B vision-language model on one plant-side GPU, so documents never leave the plant. If the model is down, a keyword-only fallback shows the right source pages with a banner.
Q11. Who is accountable for an AI-drafted work instruction?
The human approver. Nothing becomes effective without a signed approval, every AI change is shown as a diff, and planted-fault audits measure whether reviewers are really checking.
Q12. How fast is it?
Release gate: 95% of queries answered in under 15 s on a single plant-side GPU (faster with a cloud API). Simple lookups take one pass; only multi-step questions run the agent loop (Adaptive-RAG shows this keeps accuracy at under half the latency). Report the measured p50 and p95.
Q13. What about expert knowledge that isn't written down?
Expert answers to escalations are stored as advisory notes with author, date, expiry and an SOP-conflict check. Repeated notes on the same topic trigger a change-control draft, so tacit knowledge becomes an official revision.
Q14. How is this different from commercial copilots?
General copilots don't understand effectivity, machine variants, hazard tiers or change control. M.A.C.H. is built around document-control rules from ISO 9001 §7.5 and GMP, and it proves its behaviour on trap questions.
Q15. What's the business value for LTTS?
Fewer quality escapes from obsolete instructions, faster answers on night shifts without experts, audit-ready traceability, and faster WI authoring. It is reusable across LTTS's manufacturing, automotive and medical-device clients.
Q16. Hasn't HoH or SOPRAG already solved this?
HoH (ACL 2025) shows outdated documents hurt RAG but treats a document as a whole; it has no effectivity dates, ECN overlays or step-level supersession. SOPRAG (preprint) improves SOP retrieval but has no revisions, images, citations or abstention. We cite both, borrow HoH's outdated-versus-current setup, and test exactly what they leave out.

10. Honest limitations

  • The shop-floor documents are synthetic and the real revision chains come from aviation, so absolute accuracy won't transfer directly to a given plant; relative gains over baselines are the claim.
  • Conformal guarantees hold only while new questions resemble the calibration set; we mitigate with recalibration on revision events, not eliminate the risk.
  • Several of the newest supporting results are arXiv preprints and are labelled as such. Venue status of ColPali (ICLR 2025) and some others should be confirmed before citing in a paper.
  • The literature search used the OpenAlex index (covers IEEE incl. Early Access, ACM, ScienceDirect in press, Springer) but paywalled full texts were not read, so novelty claims are phrased as "we found no prior work".
