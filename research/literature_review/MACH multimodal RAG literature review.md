# Research leaves M.A.C.H.'s hardest problems open

## Executive summary

Recent peer-reviewed research supports most of the parts M.A.C.H. is built from, but nobody has put them together for shop-floor SOPs. That combination is the team's best novelty opening. Three findings hold up well. First, retrievers that look at page images beat OCR-text pipelines on documents full of diagrams and tables: VisRAG (ICLR 2025) reports a **20–40% end-to-end gain over text-based RAG** ([Yu et al.](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/3640a1997a4c9571cea9db2c82e1fc35-Abstract-Conference.html)), and screenshot retrieval beats BM25 by **17 points top-1** (EMNLP 2024) ([Ma et al.](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.373)). Second, citation-trained models and calibrated abstention work better than prompting alone, and conformal methods (ICML 2024) can put a stated bound on error ([Mohri & Hashimoto](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/mohri24a.html)). Third, the evidence on agent design favours routing each query by complexity: Adaptive-RAG (NAACL 2024) comes within a point of full iterative retrieval at **about 44% of the run time** ([Jeong et al.](https://aclanthology.org/2024.naacl-long.389)).

The weak spots are just as clear. We found no peer-reviewed benchmark of multimodal SOPs or work instructions. No peer-reviewed study handles supersession at the level of single steps. No peer-reviewed study measures hallucination when generative RAG answers from manufacturing SOPs. No peer-reviewed study tests LLM-drafted work instructions inside a formal change-control workflow. The newest results on outdated documents are arXiv preprints. They are alarming: one outdated document overturns **17–91% of otherwise-correct answers** ([Ahmed et al., arXiv, not peer-reviewed](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.31342)). Telling the model explicitly *when* the old evidence stopped applying almost fixes this in larger models.

M.A.C.H.'s strongest claims are, in order: revision-safe retrieval with a check that one answer never mixes revisions; a small public multimodal SOP benchmark; step-level citation metrics that include misgrounding and safety-step completeness; safety-tiered calibrated abstention and escalation; and evaluating drafted work instructions by what reviewers catch, not by fluency. Every "first" claim should be worded as "we found no prior work", because the search tools were limited (see the next section).

## 1. Multimodal retrieval: page images beat OCR text, but no SOP benchmark exists

### State of the art

The main shift since 2024 is that retrievers now **embed the page image directly** instead of running OCR and searching the text. ColPali uses "late interaction" (it matches many small image patches against the query words). On the ViDoRe benchmark it scores **81.3 average nDCG@5, against 65.5 for OCR+BM25 and 67.0 for captioning+BGE-M3**. It also indexes at **0.39 s/page against 7.22 s/page** for the parsing pipeline ([Faysse et al., Tables 2 and 5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.01449v6)). ColPali's peer-review status needs care. One research strand found ICLR 2025 slides for it ([ICLR 2025 slides](https://www.iclr.cc/media/iclr-2025/Slides/27679_RMItJE2.pdf)), but the arXiv page names no venue, so verify before citing it as ICLR 2025. The confirmed peer-reviewed anchors are VisRAG (ICLR 2025), which generates straight from page images and reports a 20–40% end-to-end gain over text RAG ([Yu et al.](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/3640a1997a4c9571cea9db2c82e1fc35-Abstract-Conference.html)), and DSE (EMNLP 2024). DSE beats BM25 by 17 points top-1 on 1.3M Wikipedia screenshots and beats OCR text retrieval by **more than 15 nDCG@10** on slides ([Ma et al.](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.373)).

The reason text-only RAG loses on these documents is well documented. OHRBench (ICCV 2025, peer-reviewed) used 8,561 page images from 7 domains and found that **no OCR solution tested was good enough to build a high-quality RAG knowledge base**. Both semantic and formatting noise cut RAG quality ([Zhang et al.](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2025/html/Zhang_OCR_Hinders_RAG_Evaluating_the_Cascading_Impact_of_OCR_on_ICCV_2025_paper.html)). InduOCRBench extends this to 11 hard industrial document types and finds that a low character error rate does not predict good retrieval ([Sun et al., arXiv, not peer-reviewed](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2605.00911)).

The best results in 2026 come from **combining** visual and text retrieval, not from replacing text. ViDoRe V3 (26k pages, 3,099 human-checked queries) found that fusing visual and text retrievers was "the most robust approach", and that reranking helps a lot ([Loison et al., arXiv, not peer-reviewed](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2601.08620)). MMDocIR (documents averaging 65 pages, including an "Admin & Industry" domain) shows ColPali at about **80.8 R@5 against about 72.3** for the best text retriever. It also shows that text retrievers do much better on VLM-written page descriptions than on OCR text (layout-level R@1 rises from 19.8 to 31.3) ([Dong et al., arXiv, not peer-reviewed](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.08828v3)). Parsing is not dead: MultiDocFusion builds section hierarchies from long industrial documents and reports **+8–15% retrieval precision** ([Shin et al.](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2604.12352)). Its DOI points to EMNLP 2025 main, so it is probably peer-reviewed, but the DOI was not resolved in this session. MM-BizRAG and MIDR (both arXiv, not peer-reviewed) also argue for richer indexes than page images alone ([MM-BizRAG](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2606.04231); [MIDR](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.01316)).

Peer-reviewed **industrial** RAG is still mostly text- or ontology-based. The clearest example is a voice-controlled maintenance assistant in *Computers in Industry* (2025). Fusing exact matching and vector matching reached **F1 0.611 / F2 0.704, against 0.45 / 0.511** for the best single method. Vector-only matching had precision of about 0.006 ([Ludwig et al.](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0166361525000545)). A quality-control RAG system appeared in *Advanced Engineering Informatics* (2025), but its results could not be retrieved ([Heredia Álvaro & Barreda](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1016/j.aei.2024.103007)). Truly multimodal industrial systems appear only in 2026 preprints. A Cessna 172 maintenance-manual system reaches **Recall@5 93.37% but takes 11.93 s to retrieve** ([Ha et al., arXiv, not peer-reviewed](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.18465)). ORNL's BLUEPRINT uses detectors for title blocks and parts lists plus BM25+dense hybrid search over about 770k legacy drawings, and scores nDCG@3 0.626 against 0.527 for the best VLM baseline ([Seefried et al., arXiv, not peer-reviewed](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2602.13345)).

### Gaps

**No public, peer-reviewed multimodal benchmark of shop-floor SOPs or work instructions was found.** The general benchmarks include only a small or unstated industrial share, so their gains may not carry over to exploded views, wiring diagrams or torque tables. Finding evidence *inside* a page is still poor. In ViDoRe V3, human evidence-localisation F1 was **0.602 against 0.089** for a 30B VLM ([Loison et al.](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2601.08620)). Layout-level retrieval tops out near 63 R@10 ([MMDocIR](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.08828v3)). No paper treats explicit cross-references ("see Fig. 3, step 4", "torque per Table 2", callout numbers) as a retrieval signal. GEMS-RAG and HAM-RAG point at the problem but are preprints without reported numbers ([GEMS-RAG](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.33277); [HAM-RAG](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.14032)). We found no study of query-by-photo from the shop floor, no peer-reviewed automotive multimodal RAG paper, and no study of keeping a visual index current across revisions. Exact per-domain numbers for VisRAG, M3DocRAG and OHRBench were not in the fetched abstracts.

### Novelty opportunities for M.A.C.H.

The evidence supports a **three-channel retriever**. One channel does exact matching on identifiers (part, tool and SOP numbers), following Ludwig et al. A second runs BM25 plus dense search over layout-aware chunks that include VLM captions of figures and tables, following MMDocIR. A third is a ColPali-style page-image index. The scores are fused with weights tuned on a dev set, then reranked, and page images are passed to the generator. Three additions would be new: following cross-reference edges between steps, figures and tables after the first retrieval; returning the cited page with the supporting **region highlighted**, aimed at the 0.089-vs-0.602 grounding gap; and reporting results **separately for scanned and born-digital SOPs**. A sub-second first-stage latency target is also worth stating, since the published maintenance-manual system takes about 12 s.

## 2. Citations, faithfulness and abstention: trained attribution plus conformal gates

### State of the art

The standard way to measure citations comes from **ALCE** (EMNLP 2023, peer-reviewed). *Citation recall* asks whether the cited passages, taken together, entail the statement, as judged by an NLI model. *Citation precision* asks whether each citation is actually needed ([Gao et al.](https://preview.aclanthology.org/setup/2023.emnlp-main.398)). Even the best models then lacked full citation support about half the time on ELI5 (R – verify before quoting). The most reliable citations come from models **trained** to attribute. Self-RAG (ICLR 2024) learns "reflection tokens" that judge relevance and support, and it favours precision over recall: about 70 precision / 71 recall on ASQA against ChatGPT's 65 / 77 (R – verify before quoting) ([Asai et al.](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2024/poster/18095)). Trust-Align (ICLR 2025) trains grounded answering, citation and **refusal** together, and improved **26 of 27 models**. LLaMA-3-8B gained +12.56 on ASQA, +36.04 on QAMPARI and +17.69 on ELI5 over the FRONT baseline ([Song et al.](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/4c88827decab6c046b881a2c3a99c76f-Abstract-Conference.html); [arXiv 2409.11242](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.11242v3)).

Automatic checkers are themselves noisy. AttributionBench (Findings of ACL 2024) found that **even a fine-tuned GPT-3.5 reaches only about 80% macro-F1** when judging whether evidence supports an answer ([Li et al.](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-acl.886)). RAGAS (EACL 2024 demo) gives reference-free faithfulness scores ([Es et al.](https://preview.aclanthology.org/naacl24-info/2024.eacl-demo.16)). ARES (NAACL 2024) combines small trained judges with a few hundred human labels, using prediction-powered inference (PPI), to produce **confidence intervals** instead of single scores ([Saad-Falcon et al.](https://preview.aclanthology.org/setup/2024.naacl-long.20)). RAGTruth (ACL 2024) provides span-level hallucination labels for training detectors ([Niu et al.](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.585)).

Methods for deciding when to abstain fall into three families. Learned gates include Self-RAG's support tokens, Trust-Align's refusal training and Corrective RAG's retrieval evaluator. Corrective RAG grades retrieval as correct, incorrect or ambiguous and acts on the grade ([Yan et al., arXiv, not peer-reviewed](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.15884)). Do not confuse it with the CRAG *benchmark* in Section 6. Sampling-based uncertainty includes **semantic entropy** (*Nature* 2024), which detects confabulations by grouping sampled answers by meaning. Its average AUROC is about 0.79 against about 0.69–0.70 for baselines (R – verify before quoting) ([Farquhar et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11186750)). The third family, conformal wrappers, is the only one with **finite-sample, distribution-free guarantees**. Conformal factuality (ICML 2024) drops low-confidence sub-claims until the rest is correct with a chosen probability ([Mohri & Hashimoto](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/mohri24a.html)). C-RAG (ICML 2024) gives certified upper bounds on RAG generation risk ([Kang et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.03181)). DeepMind's conformal abstention uses self-consistency "match counts" and reached a test loss of **0.065 against 0.084** for log-probability scores at α=0.1, with less abstention ([Abbasi-Yadkori et al., arXiv, not peer-reviewed; a NeurIPS 2024 listing exists, likely a workshop](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.01563)). The TACL 2025 abstention survey frames the field and its metrics, such as risk–coverage curves. It warns that over-abstention is an open problem ([Wen et al.](https://transacl.org/index.php/tacl/article/view/7077)). One strand confirmed the TACL venue and DOI; another saw only the arXiv page.

Visual citation is weak. VISA (ACL 2025, peer-reviewed) asks a VLM to draw a box around the supporting region. Zero-shot Qwen2-VL-72B reached **1.5% box accuracy**. Fine-tuning raised this to 54–68%, but cross-domain transfer collapsed to 0.2–27.8% ([Ma et al.](https://preview.aclanthology.org/setup/2025.acl-long.1456)). Two 2026 preprints confirm the pattern. In DocAttriBench, models over 30B stay below 40% Box F1 zero-shot ([De Grandis et al., arXiv, not peer-reviewed](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.20574)). CiteVQA names "attribution hallucination": GPT-5.4 answers 87.1% correctly, but only **59.0% are correct with correct evidence** ([Ma et al., arXiv, not peer-reviewed](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.12882)).

Safety-critical domain studies show that citations do not remove hallucination. Commercial legal RAG tools hallucinated on **17–33% of queries** once "misgrounded" citations (real text that does not support the claim) were counted (*Journal of Empirical Legal Studies* 2025) ([Magesh et al.](https://nlp.stanford.edu/~manning/papers/Magesh-Hallucination%E2%80%90Free-2025.pdf)). In medicine, 50–90% of LLM answers were not fully supported by their own sources (*Nature Communications* 2025; R – verify before quoting) ([Wu et al.](https://law.stanford.edu/publications/an-automated-framework-for-assessing-how-well-llms-cite-relevant-medical-references/)).

### Gaps

**The peer-reviewed industrial work found evaluates retrieval only.** SEASONED (Procedia CIRP 2025) reports maintenance work-order retrieval at MRR 0.80 against 0.64 for Ada-002 and 0.36 for BM25, but measures no generation or hallucination, and its reported improvement percentages contradict each other ([Siouffi et al.](https://orbilu.uni.lu/bitstream/10993/65242/1/1-s2.0-S2212827125006158-main.pdf)). We found no citation precision/recall study on procedural documents, no conformal guarantee for multimodal answers, no abstention calibration under document versioning, and no safety-tiered (per-hazard-class) calibration for RAG. Conformal guarantees also assume calibration and live data are exchangeable. A new machine or a new SOP revision breaks that assumption.

### Novelty opportunities for M.A.C.H.

M.A.C.H. can **extend ALCE to ordered procedures**. That means scoring support for each step, order fidelity (no skipped or reordered steps), and safety-step completeness (every PPE, LOTO or warning step in the source appears in the answer). It can adopt the legal "misgrounded" definition and add metadata checks: a citation is valid only if the text supports the claim **and** the machine model, revision and plant match. Instead of drawing free boxes, it can cite **pre-parsed element IDs** (page, figure, table, step) and have the VLM choose among them, which turns grounding into classification as DocAttriBench does. It can also stack gates: a retrieval-sufficiency check (escalate, never fall back to the web), a per-step support check, a sampling-uncertainty score, and a conformal threshold. When only part of an answer is certain, it can return the certified steps plus "escalate for steps X–Y", the conformal back-off idea applied to procedures.

## 3. Supersession and versioning: validity statements beat dates

### State of the art

The peer-reviewed knowledge-conflict survey (EMNLP 2024) separates context–memory conflicts from **inter-context conflicts**, where retrieved passages disagree with each other. Rev B against Rev C is an inter-context conflict ([Xu et al.](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.486/)). Models are highly persuadable by coherent evidence, even when it contradicts what they know, and they show confirmation bias when evidence is mixed (ICLR 2024) ([Xie et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13300v4)). RealTime QA (NeurIPS 2023) found that GPT-3 with a stale retrieval corpus did no better than no retrieval (43.9% against 44.9%). It also found that **34% of errors were retrieval errors**, so getting the right document matters most ([Kasai et al.](https://keisuke-sakaguchi.github.io/assets/pdf/2023_neurips_realtimeqa.pdf)). FreshQA (Findings of ACL 2024) showed that putting date-ordered evidence in the prompt raised GPT-3.5 from 26.0% to 56.0% (STRICT). The paper's own figures for GPT-4's gain disagree with each other (+47 vs +49.0 points) ([Vu et al.](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-acl.813.pdf)). Context-aware decoding (NAACL 2024) gives +14.3% factuality for LLaMA without training. It pushes the model toward *whatever* is in the context, so it helps only after the context has been filtered to the right revision ([Shi et al.](https://aclanthology.org/2024.naacl-short.69/)). For updates, RAG consistently beats unsupervised fine-tuning, even for brand-new facts (EMNLP 2024) ([Ovadia et al.](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.15/)).

The two results closest to M.A.C.H. are preprints. Stale-Document Poisoning (317 verified knowledge reversals, 12 models) found that outdated retrieval flips **30–37%** of correct answers. "Follow the document" instructions raise this to 66–75%, and across domains the range is **17–91%**. Dates alone help only a little, and a recency-aware re-ranker cuts poisoning by just **4.6–10.0 points**. But **explicitly stating when the old evidence stops applying** makes larger models switch "almost perfectly" ([Ahmed et al., arXiv, not peer-reviewed](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.31342)). VersionRAG builds a Category → Document → Version → Content graph with change nodes and filters vector search by version. It reaches **90% against 58% for naive RAG and 64% for GraphRAG**, using 97% fewer indexing tokens than GraphRAG. Most remaining errors come from misreading which version the query wants ([Huwiler et al., arXiv, not peer-reviewed](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2510.08109)).

Engineering change management supplies the concepts. NIST IR 7922 (a government technical report, not a journal paper) defines change requests, change orders, **effectivity** (the period a specification applies), and versions as distinct from variants. It warns against building products from "accidental mixes of original and changed parts" ([Bock & Barnard Feeney](https://tsapps.nist.gov/publication/get_pdf.cfm?pub_id=913323)). ISO 9001:2015 §7.5.3 requires the current document to be available "where and when needed". Auditors treat an obsolete work instruction at a workstation as a nonconformity, even when the master list is correct. This summary comes from a study guide, not the standard's text ([study guide](https://open-exam-prep.com/study-guides/cqi-irca-qms-lead-auditor/clauses-6-7/clause-7-documented-info)). 21 CFR 211.100 requires that deviations be "recorded and justified" ([Cornell LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/21/211.100)).

### Gaps

**Every benchmark found treats time as dates on facts (news, software, law, medicine), not as document revisions with effectivity.** None models step-level changes, scoped temporary deviations, or engineering change notices (ECNs) that change one step. VersionQA is software-only and has just 100 questions. We found no peer-reviewed paper linking ECR/ECO/ECN data to a RAG assistant, and no evidence on how models handle superseded *diagrams*. Several manufacturing knowledge-graph and ECM papers (*Journal of Manufacturing Systems* 2023/2024, *AEI* 2023, *Computers & Industrial Engineering* 2022) are **title/venue from search result only** and need confirming. The ECM knowledge-graph paper by Yin, Sun and Lu is an SSRN preprint, not peer-reviewed.

### Novelty opportunities for M.A.C.H.

Five design ideas follow from this evidence. First, **effectivity-gated retrieval as a hard filter**. Each chunk carries `doc_id`, `revision`, `status`, `effective_from`/`effective_to`, scope (site, line, product, serial range) and `superseded_by`, and the filter runs before ranking. This turns the ISO "point-of-use" rule into a property of retrieval. Second, **supersession-statement injection**, such as "Rev B was superseded by Rev C on DATE via ECN-123; do not use for execution". Third, a **revision-coherence check** after generation that blocks any answer whose citations come from more than one effective revision set. We found no paper that measures this "never mix" property. Fourth, model **deviations as scoped, time-limited overrides of single steps**, not as competing documents. Fifth, default to "effective now at this site and line" instead of asking the LLM to guess the version. That avoids VersionRAG's main failure point.

## 4. Agentic RAG and expert escalation: route first, defer by cost

### State of the art

Among peer-reviewed designs, IRCoT (ACL 2023) turns each reasoning step into a new retrieval query and gains up to **+21 retrieval and +15 QA points** on multi-hop benchmarks ([Trivedi et al.](https://aclanthology.org/2023.acl-long.557)). FLARE (EMNLP 2023) retrieves again only when the draft contains low-confidence tokens ([Jiang et al.](https://preview.aclanthology.org/setup/2023.emnlp-main.495)). The best documented trade-off is **Adaptive-RAG** (NAACL 2024). A small classifier sends each query to no retrieval, single-step retrieval or iterative retrieval. With GPT-3.5 it scored F1 50.91 against IRCoT's 50.87, at **1.46× against 3.33×** the run time of single-step retrieval. Its router was only 54.5% accurate, so a better router has room to improve on this ([Jeong et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.14403)). A 2026 comparison found that a full LLM agent used **3.3× the input tokens and 1.5× the latency** of an enhanced fixed pipeline. In **53% of cases** its second retrieval returned identical results, and it collapsed on FEVER intent routing (49.3 against 87.9) ([Ferrazzi et al., arXiv, not peer-reviewed](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2601.07711)).

On handing off to humans, learning-to-defer (L2D) decides "answer or defer" **together with the expert's accuracy and cost**. It does not just use a confidence cut-off ([Mozannar & Sontag, ICML 2020](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v119/mozannar20b/mozannar20b.pdf)). A multi-expert extension fits routing to electrical, mechanical or quality specialists, but its venue was not confirmed ([Mao, Mohri et al.](https://cs.nyu.edu/~mohri/pub/def.pdf)). Selective QA (ACL 2020) shows that calibrators help under domain shift, and new machines are exactly such a shift ([Kamath et al.](https://aclanthology.org/2020.acl-main.503)). The most demo-ready template is a NeurIPS 2025 cascade: small LLM → large LLM → human. It is calibrated on **only 100 samples**, and its thresholds update online from expert labels. Benefits were large on ARC but **not significant on MedQA/MedMCQA**, the "human" was a simulated oracle, and regret rose as expert labels got noisier ([Fanconi & van der Schaar](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/10e0c427408ccc6e073d9464e2280f89-Paper-Conference.pdf)). Another NeurIPS 2024 paper suggests handing the human a **set of candidate answers** built to maximise team accuracy ([De Toni et al.](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/37d4d4413b7c7558cc27a6d3d42ea998-Paper-Conference.pdf)).

Real-factory evidence is thin. The best peer-reviewed case is a detergent-factory RAG tool (*Frontiers in AI* 2024). On a 20-question benchmark, GPT-4 scored **97.5 factuality with 0 hallucination**, and Mixtral reached 92.5. That benchmark had one coder and no production-line test. Operators often found it "faster and easier to ask an expert colleague", the tool mattered most on **night shifts**, and a manager warned that inadequate answers "risk safety" ([Kernan Freire et al.](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/artificial-intelligence/articles/10.3389/frai.2024.1293084/pdf)). Its knowledge-capture loop has four steps: a five-why report, an LLM consistency check, expert review, and indexing separately from the manuals. A two-year follow-up reports a contribution burden, surveillance fears and operator–manager tension ([Kernan Freire et al., arXiv, not peer-reviewed](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.20192v1)).

### Gaps

We found no peer-reviewed benchmark of agentic RAG on manufacturing multi-hop questions. All the numbers above come from open-domain Wikipedia QA. ReAct and multi-agent RAG frameworks were not located with a tool, so they are not cited. No L2D study uses real industrial experts or free-text RAG answers. No controlled field trial reports task time or first-time-fix rate with and without an assistant. No study measures knowledge-base drift after expert answers are added. Two *Advanced Engineering Informatics* 2025 troubleshooting papers and several Springer chapters were found, but their results could not be retrieved.

### Novelty opportunities for M.A.C.H.

M.A.C.H. can route every query to one of **four paths: direct lookup, single retrieval, multi-hop loop, or escalate to an SME**. Escalation would trigger on calibrated uncertainty multiplied by step criticality, and the threshold would move with **expert availability**, which is the L2D expert cost made concrete (lower on night shifts). The team would report a risk–coverage curve and **expert minutes per 100 queries**. The escalation ticket would carry the query, the evidence and a ranked list of candidate answers. Each expert reply would be stored as a dated, expiring "expert note" with provenance, ranked below official SOPs, and reused as a calibration label. Capping agent loops and checking that each new retrieval adds new evidence is a cheap fix for the 53% wasted-retrieval finding.

## 5. Work-instruction drafting under change control: almost untested

### State of the art

Peer-reviewed work on LLM-*generated* work instructions is small, recent, and mostly conference papers. Meyer et al. (IEEE ETFA 2024) is one of the first tests of LLMs writing assembly instructions ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/etfa61755.2024.10710806)). Widulle et al. (IEEE INDIN 2024) let reinforcement learning fix the assembly **sequence** and the LLM write the words, a useful split for keeping step order correct ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/indin58382.2024.10774545)). Leder et al. (Procedia Computer Science 2026) personalise instructions for individual workers ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.procs.2026.02.035)). Two Springer proceedings chapters constrain output to a controlled natural language and generate instructions from 3D CAD. Their authors and metrics were not retrieved, so **verify before citing** ([CNL chapter](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-032-16889-4_64); [AIssembly](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-032-09700-2_20)). A human-in-the-loop CAD-to-instruction framework matches M.A.C.H.'s draft-then-approve flow but is a preprint ([arXiv, not peer-reviewed](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.11896)). On the assistant side, Colabianchi et al. (*Computers in Industry* 2024) evaluated an LLM assistant with people doing assembly work ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compind.2024.104129)). **The detailed results of Meyer, Widulle, Colabianchi and both Springer chapters were not retrieved; read the full texts before quoting numbers.**

The rules come from outside the research literature. EU AI Act Article 14 requires that people can oversee high-risk AI, stay aware of **automation bias**, and override or reverse its output ([Art. 14](https://euaiact.com/article/14)). ISO 9001 §7.5 requires approval before issue and control of changes. The clause wording was not retrieved, so check it against ISO 9001:2015. ASD-STE100 Simplified Technical English is a controlled-language standard from aerospace ([overview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simplified_Technical_English)). Every source found that combines it with LLMs is grey literature, including one unverified "cuts slop 72.9%" marketing claim ([explainx blog](https://www.explainx.ai/blog/asd-ste100-simplified-technical-english-ai-skill-2026)).

### Gaps

**We found no peer-reviewed study of LLM drafts going through a formal document change-control or approval workflow**, so reviewer edit distance, rejection rate and time to approve are all unmeasured. No peer-reviewed study tests whether ASD-STE100 or template constraints improve the accuracy or clarity of generated procedures. The generation papers rely on small expert reviews and report no inter-annotator agreement or step-level error taxonomy. Primary GMP texts (EU GMP Annex 11 / Chapter 4, 21 CFR Part 11) were not retrieved.

### Novelty opportunities for M.A.C.H.

Generate drafts into a **schema** with fields for step, tool, torque, PPE and caution/warning, run an automated STE-style rule checker, and route each draft as a change request that is never auto-published. The novel part is the evaluation. Measure reviewer accept / minor-edit / reject rates, normalised edit distance to the approved version, and time to approve. Above all, measure the **seeded-fault detection rate**: plant a wrong torque or a missing warning and check whether reviewers catch it. That tests automation bias directly, which Article 14 names. Even a small ablation of constrained against free generation would be new evidence.

## 6. Evaluation against BM25 and text-only RAG: BM25 is a serious baseline

### State of the art

Reviewers expect **retrieval and generation to be scored separately**. RGB (AAAI 2024, peer-reviewed) tests noise robustness, negative rejection, information integration and counterfactual robustness. LLMs were weak at refusing when the context lacked the answer and at handling wrong context, which match M.A.C.H.'s abstention and outdated-SOP cases ([Chen et al.](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/29728/31250)). The CRAG benchmark (NeurIPS 2024 D&B, peer-reviewed) scores answers as accurate, missing or hallucinated, and penalises hallucination ([Yang et al.](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/1435d2d0fca85a84d83ddcb754f58c29-Paper-Datasets_and_Benchmarks_Track.pdf)). BEIR is the usual source for "BM25 is a hard-to-beat zero-shot baseline", but no tool located it this session, so **verify before citing**. The same applies to KILT, CRUD-RAG's ACM TOIS version, MMLongBench-Doc's original venue, Smucker et al. 2007 and Dror et al. 2018.

BM25 is not a straw man on lexically dense text. On text-and-table financial documents, **BM25 (R@5 0.644) beat a commercial dense embedder (0.587)**. Hybrid RRF reached 0.695 and hybrid plus reranking 0.816, and R@5 correlated r=0.980 with end-to-end answer accuracy ([Akarsu et al., arXiv, not peer-reviewed](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2604.01733)). The "Fusion Tax" paper shows that hybrid retrieval wins on average but ranks relevant documents below dense-only on **8–21% of queries**, with RRF the riskiest fusion choice ([Fusion Tax](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.30612)). Its metadata lists an IEEE Access DOI that was not resolved, so treat it as arXiv. A scaling study claims BM25 holds up as corpora grow, but gave no numbers ([arXiv, not peer-reviewed](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.26497)).

LLM judges need checking. GPT-4 agrees with humans over 80% of the time on general preferences but shows position, verbosity and self-preference bias (Zheng et al., NeurIPS 2023 D&B; the paper's URL was not retrieved, so confirm DOI/URL). On hard knowledge-heavy pairs, strong judges do **only slightly better than random** (JudgeBench, ICLR 2025) ([Tan et al.](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/9e720fce64f91114c49cfd640d821da3-Paper-Conference.pdf)). Judges also give different ratings to the same item across runs ([Rating Roulette, Findings of EMNLP 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-emnlp.1361.pdf)). Using the same model family to rank and judge inflates scores ([Balog et al., SIGIR 2025](https://krisztianbalog.com/files/sigir2025-llms.pdf)). LLM relevance labels can match human labellers for building retrieval test sets ([Thomas et al., SIGIR 2024](https://microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/large-language-models-can-accurately-predict-searcher-preferences)). Two preprints warn that gold labels themselves fail. MMLongBench-Doc has annotation errors ([arXiv, not peer-reviewed](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.03397)). Single-gold evidence under-credits correct citations when a fact appears in several places, as safety warnings do across SOPs ([arXiv, not peer-reviewed](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.26401)).

### Gaps

We found no peer-reviewed head-to-head study of BM25, dense and hybrid retrieval on technical or industrial text, and none isolating **part numbers and alphanumeric identifiers**. No study validates LLM judges for procedural correctness (step order, preconditions, warnings) or for figure-grounded faithfulness.

### Novelty opportunities for M.A.C.H.

Report results **broken down by modality** (text, table, diagram, cross-reference) to show *where* multimodality helps. Report **per-query win/loss against BM25**, not only averages, to show there is no fusion tax. Include an identifier-query stratum, which fills the part-number gap directly. Check torque values, part numbers and warnings **deterministically** by exact match, not with an LLM judge.

## Recommended evaluation protocol

**Corpus.** Use the target plant's SOPs, work instructions, drawings and manuals, with at least two revisions for some documents so supersession can be tested. Mark which pages are scanned and which are born-digital. Freeze and hash the corpus snapshot.

**Test set.** Build about 200–300 queries plus 20–40 drafting tasks. A few hundred items matches the human-label budget ARES uses ([Saad-Falcon et al.](https://preview.aclanthology.org/setup/2024.naacl-long.20)). Write queries from real operator and quality-engineer questions, plus LLM-proposed questions that a person has edited. Tag each query by evidence type (text, table, diagram, cross-page), question type (lookup, multi-hop, procedural sequence, identifier/part number) and difficulty. Include **10–15% unanswerable queries and about 10% superseded-revision traps**, in the style of RGB's counterfactual tests ([Chen et al.](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/29728/31250)). Gold labels should cover the answer, **every** supporting location (multi-gold), and for procedures the ordered steps and critical parameters. Have two domain annotators label at least 25% of items, report Cohen's or Krippendorff's κ, and settle disagreements. Split about 30/70 into dev and test, and tune prompts only on dev.

**Systems.** Compare (1) BM25 retrieval only, (2) BM25 + LLM, (3) dense text RAG over OCR/parsed text (the text-only baseline), (4) hybrid BM25 + dense, and (5) full M.A.C.H. Run ablations without visual retrieval, the agentic loop, the reranker, the version filter, abstention, and the template/STE constraints. Keep the generator, top-k and context budget the same for all systems.

**Metrics.** Use the following groups:

| Area | Metrics |
|---|---|
| Retrieval | Recall@5/10, nDCG@10 and MRR with graded, multi-gold labels; per-query win/loss vs BM25; separate scores for identifier queries and for scanned pages |
| Answers | Correctness; CRAG-style accurate / missing / hallucinated with a hallucination penalty; RAGAS-style faithfulness; ALCE-style citation precision and recall |
| Procedures | Kendall τ on step order; exact-match rate on torques, tool IDs and part numbers (checked deterministically); safety-step recall |
| Versioning | Superseded-citation rate; mixed-revision answer rate; misgrounding rate (right text, wrong revision or variant) |
| Abstention and escalation | Abstention precision/recall on unanswerable items; risk–coverage curve; escalation rate; expert minutes per 100 queries |
| Visual citation | Element-ID accuracy for figure and table citations |
| Drafting | STE/template rule violations; accept / edit / reject rate; normalised edit distance; time to approve; seeded-fault catch rate in blinded review |
| Operations | p50/p95 latency and cost per query |

**Judging.** Use an LLM judge from a **different model family** than the generator. Run it three times with the answer order shuffled. Validate it against at least 100 human-labelled items and report κ. Correct the estimates with ARES-style PPI to get confidence intervals. Never let the judge decide safety-critical values.

**Statistics.** Make paired, per-query comparisons of M.A.C.H. against each baseline, using a paired bootstrap (10k resamples) or a permutation test, and Wilcoxon for ordinal scores. Apply a Holm correction across comparisons and report effect sizes with 95% confidence intervals. The canonical IR significance-testing papers were not verified this session, so verify them before citing.

## Conclusion

The literature changes what "better than text-only RAG" should mean for M.A.C.H. Higher retrieval scores on diagram pages are expected, because the general benchmarks already predict a 10–20 point gain. Beating BM25 on average is not enough either, since BM25 is strong on identifier-heavy text. The result that would carry weight with reviewers is that M.A.C.H. **never cites a superseded revision, never drops a safety step, and escalates instead of guessing**, measured on a benchmark that did not exist before. The judging step deserves the same care, because LLM judges are least reliable exactly where shop-floor correctness matters.

There is also a gap between what the field can do and what has been tested. Almost every capability M.A.C.H. needs exists in peer-reviewed form for Wikipedia, legal or financial text. Almost none has been tested where an error has a physical cost. That makes careful, honest, small-scale evaluation the team's main contribution, more than any single model choice.

### Becker et al. (2026) read in full (PDF supplied by Sachiv, 6 Nov 2026)
Becker, Adrian, Green, Hinrichsen (2026). Evaluating feedback mechanisms in the automatic creation of assembly instructions using RAG. *Procedia CIRP* 146, 243–248 (CIRP CMS 2026), open access CC BY. doi 10.1016/j.procir.2026.03.242.
- **What they did:** naive RAG on Azure with GPT-4o and prompt chaining. It drafts assembly instructions for 5 pneumatic assemblies (18–21 parts) from a BOM/sequence PDF and a tools-to-components PDF. After each draft a human scores it on 4 criteria (completeness, understandability, free of error, appropriate sequence; 4-point Likert, separately for assembly notes and tool notes) and feeds the score back in the chat before the next draft. They compare quantitative-only feedback with quantitative plus written-reason feedback over 4 runs each (20 drafts per condition), in forward and reverse order.
- **Findings:** quality tends to rise with each feedback loop, but no draft was fully compliant after four loops (mean 5.25 and 6.75 of 8 top scores). Score-only feedback did about as well as the richer feedback. Step order was the criterion most prone to errors.
- **Overlap with M.A.C.H.:** a peer-reviewed precedent for scoring LLM-drafted work instructions on completeness, correctness and sequence. **Adopt** their 4 criteria as part of the WI-drafting rubric and cite them.
- **What it does not cover:** revisions or supersession, approval or change control, citations to the source SOP or RCA, safety-step checks, planted-fault reviewer tests, or abstention. It is a small lab study with one LLM.
- **Effect on claims:** the reviewed-drafting claim stands if worded as "drafts produced under change control, measured by whether reviewers catch planted faults and whether safety steps survive". Do not claim novelty for "evaluating the quality of LLM-drafted assembly instructions"; Becker et al., Herbort et al. (AHFE 2025) and Jonek & Manns already do that.
